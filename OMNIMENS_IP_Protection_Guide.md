## WHAT HAPPENS IF SOMEONE STEALS YOUR TECHNOLOGY

### If a Private Company Steals It:

1. **Copyright Infringement Lawsuit** — You can sue for up to $150,000 per work infringed (statutory damages) plus your attorney's fees. Your copyright registration is required to sue.
2. **Trade Secret Misappropriation** — Under the Defend Trade Secrets Act (DTSA), you can sue in federal court. Damages include actual loss + unjust enrichment, or a reasonable royalty. Willful misappropriation allows exemplary damages up to 2x actual damages.
3. **Patent Infringement** — Once your patents are granted, you can sue for damages plus injunctive relief (court order to stop using your technology).
4. **DMCA Violations** — If they remove your tracking beacons or watermarks, that violates DMCA Section 1201. Up to $2.5 million per violation.
5. **Computer Fraud and Abuse Act (CFAA)** — If they accessed your systems without authorization to obtain the technology.

### If the Government Takes More Rights Than Allowed:

1. **Court of Federal Claims** — You can sue the U.S. government for IP infringement in the United States Court of Federal Claims (28 U.S.C. § 1498)
2. **Bayh-Dole Violations** — If the government takes ownership of inventions in violation of the Bayh-Dole Act, you can challenge this in court
3. **Contract Disputes Act** — If the government breaches the IP terms of your contract
4. **The Tucker Act** — Allows monetary claims against the U.S. government

**The government CAN be sued for IP theft.** The Court of Federal Claims exists specifically for this purpose.

### To Be Able to Sue, You NEED:

- Registered copyrights (Steps 1-2)
- Filed patents (Step 3)
- Registered trademarks (Steps 4-5)
- Documented trade secret measures (Step 7)
- Timestamped evidence of creation (Steps 6, 9)
- Properly marked documents (Step 13)

**Without these, your ability to recover damages is severely limited. With them, you have a complete legal arsenal.**
