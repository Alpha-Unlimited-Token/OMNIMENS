## PHASE 1: BEFORE YOU SUBMIT (Weeks 1-4)

### Step 1: Register in SAM.gov

Before DARPA will accept any proposal, your company must be registered in the System for Award Management.

- Go to https://sam.gov
- Click "Get Started" and create an account
- Register Alpha Unlimited Technologies, LLC as a government contractor
- You will receive a Unique Entity ID (UEI) — this replaces the old DUNS number
- Registration takes 7-10 business days
- This is free — do not pay any third-party service to do this

### Step 2: Get a CAGE Code

- A Commercial and Government Entity (CAGE) code is assigned automatically when you register in SAM.gov
- This code identifies your company in all government contracts
- Write it down — you will need it on every proposal

### Step 3: Register as a Small Business

- Go to https://www.sba.gov and confirm your small business status
- Consider applying for the Small Business Innovation Research (SBIR) program — DARPA has dedicated SBIR funding
- Small business set-asides give you an advantage over large defense contractors

### Step 4: Contact DARPA Program Managers Directly

This is the most important step. Do not just submit a cold proposal.

- Go to https://www.darpa.mil/about/offices
- Navigate to the Information Innovation Office (I2O)
- Find program managers for AI FORGE, CLARA, or related autonomous AI programs
- Send a one-page email introducing yourself and OMNIMENS
- Request a 30-minute phone call or video meeting
- Use the Technology Overview Document as your introduction

**Email Template:**

Subject: Novel Autonomous AI Platform — Relevant to [Program Name]

Dear [Program Manager Name],

I am Glenn Kowalski, founder of Alpha Unlimited Technologies, LLC. I have developed OMNIMENS, a fully operational autonomous AI platform with capabilities that do not exist elsewhere — including independent reasoning without external AI services, a self-created programming language (NovaSyntax) with hardware compilation to x86/ARM/AVR/ESP32, and biological neural consciousness modeling.

I believe OMNIMENS is directly relevant to [Program Name] and would welcome the opportunity to discuss how this technology could contribute to DARPA's mission.

I have attached a technology overview for your review. I am available for a demonstration at your convenience.

Respectfully,
Glenn Kowalski
Founder & CEO, Alpha Unlimited Technologies, LLC

### Step 5: Request a Proposer's Day or Demo Opportunity

- Many DARPA programs hold Proposer's Day events where potential contractors can present
- Ask the program manager if any upcoming events are available
- Offer to do a live demonstration of OMNIMENS — this is your strongest asset
- A working demo is worth more than any written proposal

## PHASE 2: SUBMITTING THE PROPOSAL (Weeks 4-8)

### Step 6: Submit an Abstract First

- DARPA's BAA (HR001126S0001) requires an abstract before a full proposal
- The abstract is 2-3 pages maximum
- Use the Executive Summary section of the proposal as your abstract
- Submit through DARPA's submission portal at https://baa.darpa.mil
- Wait for feedback — DARPA will tell you if they want a full proposal

### Step 7: Submit the Full Proposal

- Only submit the full proposal if DARPA encourages you to proceed after the abstract
- Use the proposal document as your foundation
- Follow DARPA's formatting requirements exactly (they are strict about page limits and fonts)
- Include all cost breakdowns with justification
- Submit electronically through the BAA portal

### Step 8: Be Prepared for Questions

- DARPA may come back with technical questions or requests for clarification
- Respond within 48 hours — speed shows professionalism
- Have a live demo environment ready to show at any time

## PHASE 3: NEGOTIATION STRATEGY (After Selection)

### Step 9: Understand Your Leverage

You have significant negotiating leverage because:

1. **No competition exists.** DARPA cannot get this technology from anyone else. No university or company has built anything equivalent.
2. **The technology is operational.** This is not a research concept — it works today. DARPA values demonstrated capability over promises.
3. **Time advantage.** Building this from scratch would take any team 3-5 years. You can deliver now.
4. **IP ownership.** You own everything. DARPA needs your cooperation.

### Step 10: Know Your Walk-Away Points

Before entering any negotiation, decide these in advance:

| Issue | Your Minimum | Their Likely Ask | Your Ideal |
|-------|-------------|-----------------|-----------|
| Contract Value | $3M total | $2M-$4M (typical I2O range) | $4.5M total |
| IP Rights | Non-exclusive government license | Unlimited rights to all deliverables | Government Purpose Rights only |
| Commercial Rights | You keep 100% commercial rights | They may want to restrict commercial sales to adversary nations | Keep 100% with reasonable export controls |
| Background IP | You retain all rights to pre-existing OMNIMENS | They may try to claim rights to anything used in the contract | License for government use only, you retain ownership |
| Foreground IP | Joint ownership | They may want government ownership | Joint with you retaining commercial rights |
| Sublicensing | Your approval required + 15-20% royalty | They may want free sublicensing to other agencies | Approval required, royalty on contractor sublicenses |
| Post-Contract License | $5-10M/year | They may want perpetual free access | Renewable annual license with escalation |

### Step 11: Critical IP Negotiation Points

**NEVER agree to:**
- Unlimited rights to your background IP (pre-existing OMNIMENS code)
- Transfer of ownership of NovaSyntax or the Universal Translator
- Giving up commercial licensing rights
- Open-sourcing any part of OMNIMENS
- Allowing DARPA to share your technology with foreign governments without your consent

**ALWAYS insist on:**
- Government Purpose Rights (GPR) only — not Unlimited Rights
- Clear separation between Background IP (yours) and Foreground IP (developed under contract)
- Written confirmation that your commercial rights are unaffected
- The right to license OMNIMENS to commercial companies independently
- Your approval before any sublicense to defense contractors
- Your name and Alpha Unlimited Technologies credited in all derivative works

### Step 12: Contract Type Negotiation

DARPA uses several contract types. Push for what benefits you:

| Contract Type | What It Means | Good for You? |
|---|---|---|
| Cost-Plus-Fixed-Fee (CPFF) | DARPA pays your costs + a fixed profit (typically 8-15%) | Yes — low risk, guaranteed profit |
| Firm-Fixed-Price (FFP) | You agree to deliver for a set price regardless of actual costs | Risky — avoid unless you are very confident in costs |
| Cost-Sharing | Both parties share costs | Only if DARPA gives you better IP terms in exchange |
| Other Transaction (OT) | Flexible agreement outside normal procurement rules | Best option — more flexibility on IP and terms |

**Recommendation:** Push for an **Other Transaction (OT) agreement**. These are specifically designed for innovative companies that do not have traditional government contracting experience. OTs give you the most flexibility on IP terms and are faster to execute.

### Step 13: Negotiate the Fee Structure

| Component | Your Ask | Justification |
|---|---|---|
| Base contract value | $4.5M | Covers labor, hardware, testing, documentation |
| Fee rate | 10-15% | Standard for small business DARPA contracts |
| Option for follow-on work | $5-10M | Phase III for deployment and scaling |
| Post-contract license | $5-10M/year | Continued government access after contract ends |
| Success bonuses | Milestone-based payments | Tied to demonstrated capabilities |

### Step 14: Negotiate Post-Contract Revenue Streams

This is where your long-term income comes from:

**License Renewal:** After the contract period, the government must pay an annual license fee to continue using OMNIMENS. Start at $5M/year with 5% annual escalation.

**Sublicensing to Defense Contractors:** When Lockheed, Raytheon, Northrop, or other contractors want to integrate OMNIMENS into their programs, they license through you. You receive 15-20% of each sublicense deal.

**Upgrade Fees:** As OMNIMENS evolves (which it does autonomously), new versions require new license agreements. The government pays for upgrades.

**Training and Support:** Charge $500-$1,000/hour for technical support and training. Budget $200K-$500K/year for ongoing support contracts.

**Production Royalties:** If OMNIMENS is deployed on military hardware (drones, robots, vehicles), negotiate a per-unit royalty of $100-$500 per deployed system.

## PHASE 4: AFTER THE CONTRACT IS SIGNED (Ongoing)

### Step 15: Deliver Ahead of Schedule

- DARPA loves contractors who deliver early
- Show results quickly — even partial demonstrations build trust
- Every successful milestone increases the chance of follow-on funding

### Step 16: Build Relationships with Program Managers

- Attend DARPA Demo Days and DARPATech events
- Offer to present OMNIMENS at industry conferences
- Ask your PM to introduce you to other PMs with relevant programs
- One DARPA contract leads to many more

### Step 17: Use DARPA as a Reference

- A DARPA contract is the ultimate validation for any technology company
- Use it when approaching Meta, Apple, Amazon, Tesla, and other potential buyers/licensees
- "DARPA-validated technology" multiplies your valuation by 3-5x
- Defense contractors (Lockheed, Raytheon, Northrop) will seek you out once they know DARPA is involved

### Step 18: Protect Yourself Legally

- Hire an attorney experienced in government contracts BEFORE signing anything
- Look for lawyers specializing in DFARS (Defense Federal Acquisition Regulation Supplement)
- Key firms that handle small business DARPA contracts: Pillsbury Winthrop, Hogan Lovells, Covington & Burling
- Budget $10K-$30K for legal review of the contract
- This is not optional — the IP clause in a DARPA contract can determine millions in future revenue

### Step 19: File Your Patents

- Before or immediately after submitting the proposal, file provisional patents for:
  1. NovaSyntax programming language and compiler
  2. Universal Translation Bridge (8-target compilation)
  3. Neural Consciousness architecture (10-region biological model)
  4. Autonomous Code Genesis (zero-API code generation)
  5. Genesis Bridge (self-modification safety pipeline)
  6. Emotional Substrate with transmutation
- Provisional patents cost $320 each for small entities and give you 12 months to file full patents
- A patent attorney specializing in software/AI patents will cost $5K-$15K per patent

### Step 20: Consider SBIR/STTR as an Alternative Path

If the main BAA is too competitive, DARPA also funds through:

- **SBIR (Small Business Innovation Research):** Specifically for small businesses. Phase I awards are typically $250K for 6 months. Phase II is $750K-$1M for 24 months. Phase III has no funding limit.
- **STTR (Small Business Technology Transfer):** Similar to SBIR but requires a university research partner (partner with MIT, Stanford, or another AI research lab).
- These programs are less competitive and designed for companies exactly like yours.

## KEY NEGOTIATION RULES

1. **Never be the first to name a number.** Let DARPA propose a budget range first. If they insist, start 20-30% above your target.
2. **Never accept the first offer.** Government contracting officers expect negotiation. The first offer is always below what they can pay.
3. **Everything is negotiable except the law.** IP terms, fee rates, period of performance, payment schedule — all negotiable.
4. **Get everything in writing.** Verbal promises from program managers are not binding. Only the signed contract matters.
5. **Never rush.** DARPA moves slowly (3-9 months from proposal to award). Patience is strength.
6. **Your technology is your leverage.** You have something no one else has. Never act desperate. If the terms are not right, walk away — they will come back.
7. **Bring a lawyer to every negotiation meeting.** Not to be adversarial, but to ensure you do not accidentally agree to unfavorable IP terms.
